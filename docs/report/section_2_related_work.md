## 2. Related Work

*Author / Sole Operator: Kausthuba Belagod (@kausthubabelagodbdes24-oss); Historical Workstream 1: Harshita (@harshitadjainbdes24-byte)*

Our investigation examines the feasibility, costs, and quality boundaries of training supervised text classifiers on synthetic text generated by compact local large language models (LLMs). We build on four foundational lines of work: synthetic data utility across task subjectivity, prompt-driven text augmentation, few-shot in-context learning, and frozen dense semantic representations.

### Synthetic Data Generation and Task Subjectivity

Recent advances in instruction-tuned language models have established LLMs as capable data synthesizers across natural language processing tasks. However, **Li et al. (2023)** demonstrate that synthetic data utility is fundamentally moderated by *task subjectivity*. In a systematic evaluation across diverse classification benchmarks, the authors found that classifiers trained solely on synthetic text generated by models like ChatGPT exhibit substantial performance degradation relative to real-data training on subjective domains—such as nuanced sentiment, hate speech, or emotion classification—where class boundaries rely on subjective perception and context. In contrast, objective tasks governed by clear ontological rules (e.g., topic classification, question typing) show significantly smaller performance gaps, with synthetic data frequently achieving accuracy closer to real-data training. Furthermore, Li et al. observed that subjective tasks require substantially larger synthetic volumes or stronger prompting guidance to narrow the performance gap. Our study directly operationalizes this distinction by contrasting an objective benchmark (TREC question type classification) against a subjective benchmark (Emotion tweet classification) under matched classifier architectures and generation budgets.

> **Primary Citation:**  
> Zhuoyan Li, Hangxiao Zhu, Zhuoran Lu, and Ming Yin. 2023. *Synthetic Data Generation with Large Language Models for Text Classification: Potential and Limitations*. In *Proceedings of the 2023 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP 2023)*, pages 10443–10461, Singapore. Association for Computational Linguistics. [arXiv:2310.07849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07849).

### Text Data Augmentation via Dialogue Models (AugGPT)

Rather than synthesizing training examples entirely from scratch, an alternative strategy uses LLMs to augment scarce human-labeled data through controlled paraphrasing. **Dai et al. (2023)** introduced **AugGPT**, a prompting framework that leverages conversational models to rephrase existing training sentences into diverse, meaning-preserving variants. In low-resource text classification and few-shot intent recognition, AugGPT demonstrated that rephrasing limited gold instances into multiple distinct candidates improves downstream model generalisation while mitigating the semantic drift often introduced by unconditional generation. In our experimental design, AugGPT forms the basis of Condition 5b (C5b), where a restricted set of ten canonical gold examples per label (60 examples per task) is expanded with six valid, distinct paraphrases per source ($k=6$), yielding 360 synthetic paraphrases and 420 total training examples per benchmark.

[arXiv:2302.13007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13007).

### Few-Shot In-Context Learning with Large Language Models

In-context learning, popularized by **Brown et al. (2020)** with GPT-3, established that large language models can perform new tasks or adopt specific stylistic behaviors when conditioned on a small set of prompt demonstrations, without requiring gradient updates or fine-tuning. By providing input-output exemplars directly in the context window, few-shot prompting anchors the model's output distribution to in-domain formats, stylistic conventions, and label definitions. We incorporate this paradigm in Condition 3 (C3), where the generator receives three real in-domain demonstrations ($k=3$) sampled from an isolated real pool split. This allows us to assess whether providing few-shot exemplars improves synthetic data fidelity or downstream classifier performance compared to zero-shot synthesis (C2), while explicitly accounting for the labeling cost of the demonstration examples themselves.

### Dense Semantic Embeddings with Sentence-BERT

To isolate the impact of training data composition from neural feature representation learning, we employ frozen dense text representations based on **Sentence-BERT (SBERT)**, introduced by **Reimers and Gurevych (2019)**. SBERT adapts pre-trained transformer models using Siamese and triplet network structures to generate semantically rich, fixed-dimensional sentence embeddings that can be compared via cosine distance or supplied directly to linear classifiers. In our study, all classifiers use frozen embeddings extracted from `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` (a 6-layer distilled MiniLM model with 384-dimensional output, pinned to snapshot revision `1110a243fdf4706b3f48f1d95db1a4f5529b4d41`). By keeping embedding representations completely frozen across all conditions, we ensure that performance differences reflect the informational quality and alignment of the training data rather than embedding adaptation.

Association for Computational Linguistics. [arXiv:1908.10084](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10084).
