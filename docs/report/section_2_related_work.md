## 2. Related Work

*Generation workstream owner / section author: Harshita (@harshitadjainbdes24-byte); implementation and integration performed by Kausthuba Belagod (@kausthubabelagodbdes24-oss) on her behalf.*

Our investigation examines the feasibility, costs, and quality boundaries of training supervised text classifiers on synthetic text generated by compact local large language models (LLMs). We build on four foundational lines of work: synthetic data utility across task subjectivity, prompt-driven text augmentation, few-shot in-context learning, and frozen dense semantic representations.

### Synthetic Data Generation and Task Subjectivity

**Li et al. (2023)** generated training data with a large language model for a range of text-classification tasks and studied how the subjectivity of a task relates to the performance of models trained on that synthetic data. They report that subjectivity, at both the task level and the instance level, is negatively associated with the performance of models trained on the synthetic data. Our study follows this framing by contrasting an objective benchmark (TREC question type classification) with a subjective benchmark (Emotion tweet classification) under the same classifier. It does not measure subjectivity itself, and the two tasks also differ in other ways.

> **Primary Citation:**<br>
> Zhuoyan Li, Hangxiao Zhu, Zhuoran Lu, and Ming Yin. 2023. *Synthetic Data Generation with Large Language Models for Text Classification: Potential and Limitations*. In *Proceedings of the 2023 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP 2023)*, pages 10443–10461, Singapore. Association for Computational Linguistics. [arXiv:2310.07849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07849).

### Text Data Augmentation via Dialogue Models (AugGPT)

Rather than synthesizing training examples entirely from scratch, an alternative strategy uses LLMs to augment scarce labelled data through paraphrasing. **Dai et al. (2023)** proposed **AugGPT**, which uses ChatGPT to rephrase each training sentence into several conceptually similar variants that differ in wording; the augmented samples are then used to train downstream models. The authors report that, on few-shot text classification tasks, AugGPT outperformed the augmentation methods they compared against in test accuracy and in the distribution of the augmented samples. Our study uses a local model (`qwen3:4b`) rather than ChatGPT. In our experimental design, AugGPT forms the basis of Condition 5b (C5b), where a restricted set of ten canonical gold examples per label (60 examples per task) is expanded with six valid, distinct paraphrases per source ($k=6$), yielding 360 synthetic paraphrases and 420 total training examples per benchmark.

> **Primary Citation:**<br>
> Haixing Dai, Zhengliang Liu, Wenxiong Liao, Xiaoke Huang, Yihan Cao, Zihao Wu, Lin Zhao, Shaochen Xu, Wei Liu, Ninghao Liu, Sheng Li, Dajiang Zhu, Hongmin Cai, Lichao Sun, Quanzheng Li, Dinggang Shen, Tianming Liu, and Xiang Li. 2023. *AugGPT: Leveraging ChatGPT for Text Data Augmentation*. [arXiv:2302.13007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13007).

### Few-Shot In-Context Learning with Large Language Models

In-context learning, popularized by **Brown et al. (2020)** with GPT-3, established that large language models can perform new tasks or adopt specific stylistic behaviors when conditioned on a small set of prompt demonstrations, without requiring gradient updates or fine-tuning. By providing input-output exemplars directly in the context window, few-shot prompting anchors the model's output distribution to in-domain formats, stylistic conventions, and label definitions. We incorporate this paradigm in Condition 3 (C3), where the generator receives three real in-domain demonstrations ($k=3$) sampled from an isolated real pool split. This lets us compare classifiers trained on few-shot synthetic data with those trained on zero-shot synthetic data (C2), while disclosing the labelling cost of the demonstration examples separately.

### Dense Semantic Embeddings with Sentence-BERT

To isolate the impact of training data composition from neural feature representation learning, we employ frozen dense text representations based on **Sentence-BERT (SBERT)**, introduced by **Reimers and Gurevych (2019)**. SBERT adapts pre-trained transformer models using Siamese and triplet network structures to generate semantically rich, fixed-dimensional sentence embeddings that can be compared via cosine distance or supplied directly to linear classifiers. In our study, all classifiers use frozen embeddings extracted from `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` (a 6-layer distilled MiniLM model with 384-dimensional output, pinned to snapshot revision `1110a243fdf4706b3f48f1d95db1a4f5529b4d41`). By keeping embedding representations completely frozen across all conditions, we ensure that performance differences reflect the informational quality and alignment of the training data rather than embedding adaptation.

> **Primary Citation:**<br>
> Nils Reimers and Iryna Gurevych. 2019. *Sentence-BERT: Sentence Embeddings using Siamese BERT-Networks*. In *Proceedings of the 2019 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing and the 9th International Joint Conference on Natural Language Processing (EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019)*, pages 3982–3992, Hong Kong, China. Association for Computational Linguistics. [arXiv:1908.10084](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10084).
