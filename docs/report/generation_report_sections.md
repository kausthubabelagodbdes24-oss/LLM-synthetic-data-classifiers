## 2. Related Work

*Owner: Harshita (@harshitadjainbdes24-byte); Operator: Kausthuba (@kausthubabelagodbdes24-oss)*

Our investigation examines the feasibility, costs, and quality boundaries of training supervised text classifiers on synthetic text generated by compact local large language models (LLMs). We build on four foundational lines of work: synthetic data utility across task subjectivity, prompt-driven text augmentation, few-shot in-context learning, and frozen dense semantic representations.

### Synthetic Data Generation and Task Subjectivity

Recent advances in instruction-tuned language models have established LLMs as capable data synthesizers across natural language processing tasks. However, **Li et al. (2023)** demonstrate that synthetic data utility is fundamentally moderated by *task subjectivity*. In a systematic evaluation across diverse classification benchmarks, the authors found that classifiers trained solely on synthetic text generated by models like ChatGPT exhibit substantial performance degradation relative to real-data training on subjective domains—such as nuanced sentiment, hate speech, or emotion classification—where class boundaries rely on subjective perception and context. In contrast, objective tasks governed by clear ontological rules (e.g., topic classification, question typing) show significantly smaller performance gaps, with synthetic data frequently matching or approaching real-data efficacy. Furthermore, Li et al. observed that subjective tasks require substantially larger synthetic volumes or stronger prompting guidance to narrow the performance gap. Our study directly operationalizes this distinction by contrasting an objective benchmark (TREC question type classification) against a subjective benchmark (Emotion tweet classification) under matched classifier architectures and generation budgets.

> **Primary Citation:**  
> Zhuoyan Li, Hangxiao Zhu, Zhuoran Lu, and Ming Yin. 2023. *Synthetic Data Generation with Large Language Models for Text Classification: Potential and Limitations*. In *Proceedings of the 2023 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP 2023)*, pages 10443–10461, Singapore. Association for Computational Linguistics. [arXiv:2310.07849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07849).

### Text Data Augmentation via Dialogue Models (AugGPT)

Rather than synthesizing training examples entirely from scratch, an alternative strategy uses LLMs to augment scarce human-labeled data through controlled paraphrasing. **Dai et al. (2023)** introduced **AugGPT**, a prompting framework that leverages conversational models to rephrase existing training sentences into diverse, meaning-preserving variants. In low-resource text classification and few-shot intent recognition, AugGPT demonstrated that rephrasing limited gold instances into multiple distinct candidates improves downstream model generalisation while mitigating the semantic drift often introduced by unconditional generation. In our experimental design, AugGPT forms the basis of Condition 5b (C5b), where a restricted set of ten canonical gold examples per label (60 examples per task) is expanded with six valid, distinct paraphrases per source ($k=6$), yielding 360 synthetic paraphrases and 420 total training examples per benchmark.

> **Primary Citation:**  
> Haixing Dai, Zhengliang Liu, Wenxiong Liao, Xiaoke Huang, Yihan Cao, Zihao Wu, Lin Zhao, Shaochen Xu, Wei Liu, Ninghao Liu, Sheng Li, Dajiang Zhu, Hongmin Cai, Lichao Sun, Quanzheng Li, Dinggang Shen, Tianming Liu, and Xiang Li. 2023. *AugGPT: Leveraging ChatGPT for Text Data Augmentation*. [arXiv:2302.13007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13007).

### Few-Shot In-Context Learning with Large Language Models

In-context learning, popularized by **Brown et al. (2020)** with GPT-3, established that large language models can perform new tasks or adopt specific stylistic behaviors when conditioned on a small set of prompt demonstrations, without requiring gradient updates or fine-tuning. By providing input-output exemplars directly in the context window, few-shot prompting anchors the model's output distribution to in-domain formats, stylistic conventions, and label definitions. We incorporate this paradigm in Condition 3 (C3), where the generator receives three real in-domain demonstrations ($k=3$) sampled from an isolated real pool split. This allows us to assess whether providing few-shot exemplars improves synthetic data fidelity or downstream classifier performance compared to zero-shot synthesis (C2), while explicitly accounting for the labeling cost of the demonstration examples themselves.

> **Primary Citation:**  
> Tom B. Brown, Benjamin Mann, Nick Ryder, Melanie Subbiah, Jared Kaplan, Prafulla Dhariwal, Arvind Neelakantan, Pranav Shyam, Girish Sastry, Amanda Askell, Sandhini Agarwal, Ariel Herbert-Voss, Gretchen Krueger, Tom Henighan, Rewon Child, Aditya Ramesh, Daniel M. Ziegler, Jeffrey Wu, Clemens Winter, Christopher Hesse, Mark Chen, Eric Sigler, Mateusz Litwin, Scott Gray, Benjamin Chess, Jack Clark, Christopher Berner, Sam McCandlish, Alec Radford, Ilya Sutskever, and Dario Amodei. 2020. *Language Models are Few-Shot Learners*. In *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2020)*, volume 33, pages 1877–1901. Curran Associates, Inc. [arXiv:2005.14165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165).

### Dense Semantic Embeddings with Sentence-BERT

To isolate the impact of training data composition from neural feature representation learning, we employ frozen dense text representations based on **Sentence-BERT (SBERT)**, introduced by **Reimers and Gurevych (2019)**. SBERT adapts pre-trained transformer models using Siamese and triplet network structures to generate semantically rich, fixed-dimensional sentence embeddings that can be compared via cosine distance or supplied directly to linear classifiers. In our study, all classifiers use frozen embeddings extracted from `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` (a 6-layer distilled MiniLM model with 384-dimensional output, pinned to snapshot revision `1110a243fdf4706b3f48f1d95db1a4f5529b4d41`). By keeping embedding representations completely frozen across all conditions, we ensure that performance differences reflect the informational quality and alignment of the training data rather than embedding adaptation.

> **Primary Citation:**

In *Proceedings of the 2019 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing and the 9th International Joint Conference on Natural Language Processing (EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019)*, pages 3982–3992, Hong Kong, China. Association for Computational Linguistics. [arXiv:1908.10084](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10084).

## 3a. Model and Generation Method

*Owner: Harshita (@harshitadjainbdes24-byte); Operator: Kausthuba (@kausthubabelagodbdes24-oss)*

All synthetic data for the accepted `scope30-v1` study was generated locally and offline using Ollama 0.40.13 on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 Laptop GPU (8 GiB VRAM), under Windows 11. 

Following the initial generation pilot, the original candidate generator (`llama3.2:3b`, digest `a80c4f17…`) exhibited significant label drift and high error rates on subjective categories (documented in [`docs/PILOT_REPORT.md`](../PILOT_REPORT.md)). Under [ADR 0010](../adr/0010-test-qwen-fallback-after-pilot-failure.md) and [ADR 0011](../adr/0011-accept-qwen-pilot-and-uniform-100-target.md), the project lead tested and accepted `qwen3:4b` as the replacement generator across all conditions:
- **Configured Model Tag:** `qwen3:4b`
- **Model Digest:** `359d7dd4bcdab3d86b87d73ac27966f4dbb9f5efdfcc75d34a8764a09474fae7` (pinned in `ws1_generation.generate.APPROVED_GENERATORS`)
- **Sampling Temperature:** $T = 0.9$
- **Inference Configuration:** Executed via Ollama's HTTP REST API (`/api/generate`) with JSON formatting (`format: "json"`). For zero-shot, few-shot, and standard AugGPT, model default thinking was retained. For corrected TREC AugGPT calls, thinking was explicitly disabled (`"think": false`) and output tokens were capped at `num_predict: 1024` under [ADR 0013 Amendment 1](../adr/0013-trec-auggpt-answer-type.md).
- **Provenance Tracking:** Every synthetic artifact is paired with an append-only JSON sidecar (`<setting>_seed<S>.provenance.json`) recording the model identity, resolved digest, generation fingerprint, prompt dependency hashes, exact source hashes, invocation sessions with start/finish timestamps, elapsed seconds, accepted counts, and cumulative statistics.

### Generation Prompt Strategies

We implemented three distinct prompting strategies in `ws1_generation/generate.py`:

1. **Zero-Shot (`prompt_zero_shot`)**:
   Generates synthetic text from scratch conditioned on task context, target label definitions, and boundary guidelines. The prompt incorporates:
   - System/context framing: `You are building a dataset of {context}. Write {n} different examples of {style}.`
   - Canonical label definition and boundaries: Explicit instructions distinguish easily confused classes (e.g., in TREC, requiring open search-style questions and separating `abbreviation` expansions from `description` definitions; in Emotion, requiring the target category to be the dominant emotion rather than a secondary reaction).
   - Rotating lexical nudges (`NUDGES`): Encourages diverse syntactic openings and varied sentence lengths.
   - Format enforcement: Strict JSON reply constraint: `Reply only with JSON: {"examples": ["...", "..."]}`.

2. **Few-Shot (`prompt_few_shot`)**:
   Inherits the zero-shot task framing, label boundaries, and constraints, but additionally prepends three real in-domain demonstration exemplars ($k=3$) sampled from an isolated demonstration pool split (`data/real/<task>/pool.jsonl`):
   ```
   Here are real examples with this label:
   - "..."
   - "..."
   - "..."
   Use these for style, while following the requested label's requirements above.
   Write NEW examples in the same style. Do not copy or paraphrase these.
   ```
   The pool split is strictly disjoint from both the real training split (`train.jsonl`) and the official test set (`test.jsonl`).

3. **AugGPT Paraphrasing (`prompt_auggpt` and Variants)**:
   Performs sentence-level paraphrasing on canonical low-resource real sources ($n=10$ per label from `lowres_seed13.jsonl`):
   - **Base AugGPT (`prompt_auggpt`)**:
     `Please rephrase the following sentence {k} different ways, keeping its meaning: "{text}"` requesting $k=6$ paraphrases per source example.
   - **Length Guard Variant (`prompt_auggpt_length_guard`)**:
     Sources whose whitespace word count exceeds the task's configured limit (`max_words`: 30 words for Emotion) append:
     `Each rephrasing must be at most {max_words} words, so shorten it: keep the main meaning and the writer's feeling, and leave out minor details.`
     This prevents unparseable length rejections on long source tweets.
   - **Corrected TREC Answer-Type Variant (`prompt_auggpt_trec_answer_type`, ADR 0013 v2)**:
     To eliminate label drift observed during initial quality reviews, TREC seed-13 paraphrasing supplies the source question's label and definition, and requires:
     `Every rephrasing must keep the same expected answer type, so it keeps this label. ... Keep the same facts and relationships between them, the same entities and participants (who did what to whom), any negation, and the same intent. Each rephrasing must be a question or request for information. Do not answer the question, and do not add facts, names, dates or details that are not in it.`
     It includes an explicit `human` question rule: `"Who is X?"` questions must remain who-questions about the same referent and retain the `human` label, preventing drift toward biographical description. Calls are bounded with `"think": false` and `num_predict: 1024`.

### Parsing, Filtering, and Fail-Closed Safeguards

Candidate generations are processed through a multi-stage filtering pipeline before admission to synthetic corpora:
- **Structured JSON Parsing**: Parses the model reply into an array of string candidates. If parsing fails, the call is retried up to three times.
- **Conversational Chatter Filter**: Regex patterns (`NON_EXAMPLE_PATTERNS`) reject assistant conversational filler (e.g., *"Here are your examples:"*, *"Sure, below are..."*, *"As an AI..."*).
- **Exact-Match Test Leakage Exclusion**: Every candidate is normalized (lowercased, whitespace-collapsed, punctuation-stripped) and checked against the untouched official held-out test split (`data/real/<task>/test.jsonl`). Any candidate matching a test item is immediately rejected. Across all accepted synthetic corpora, zero test leaks occurred.
- **In-Corpus Deduplication**: Duplicate normalized texts within the same artifact are rejected.
- **Copy Rejection**: Few-shot candidates copying any of the prompt's three demonstrations are rejected. AugGPT candidates identical to the source text are rejected.
- **Write-Ahead Journaling**: Each batch of accepted rows is written to an identity-bound write-ahead journal before appending to the JSONL corpus, followed by atomic provenance metadata replacement. Interrupted runs resume cleanly from the shortfall without duplicate rows or count inflation.

## 6. Limitations and Conclusions

*Owner: Harshita (@harshitadjainbdes24-byte); Operator: Kausthuba (@kausthubabelagodbdes24-oss)*

### Core Findings Grounded in the Four Accepted Comparisons

Our evaluation of the twelve-key `scope30-v1` grid on seed 13 (`docs/analysis/scope30-v1-seed13/`) yielded empirical observations across all four planned experimental comparisons, covering 1,440 total selected synthetic examples (720 selected zero/few-shot texts and 720 AugGPT paraphrases):

#### 1. Synthetic-versus-Real Gap Across Tasks (C2, C3 vs C1)

| Benchmark | Condition | C1 Real Macro-F1 | Condition Macro-F1 | Absolute Gap | Relative Gap | Accuracy | Test Items Shift |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **TREC** | C2 Zero-shot | 0.6009 | 0.6257 | +0.0247 | **+4.12%** | 0.6680 (+0.0160) | +8 questions |
| **TREC** | C3 Few-shot | 0.6009 | 0.6411 | +0.0402 | **+6.69%** | 0.6880 (+0.0360) | +18 questions |
| **Emotion** | C2 Zero-shot | 0.4082 | 0.3804 | -0.0278 | **-6.80%** | 0.4305 (-0.0115) | -23 tweets |
| **Emotion** | C3 Few-shot | 0.4082 | 0.3841 | -0.0241 | **-5.90%** | 0.4285 (-0.0135) | -27 tweets |

- **Observed Direction:** On TREC (objective), synthetic-only training sets scored higher than real-only training, exhibiting positive relative gaps (+4.12% for zero-shot, +6.69% for few-shot). On Emotion (subjective), synthetic-only training sets scored lower than real-only training, exhibiting negative relative gaps (-6.80% for zero-shot, -5.90% for few-shot).
- **Cross-Task Gap Difference:** The difference in relative gap (Emotion minus TREC) is **-10.92 percentage points** for zero-shot (C2) and **-12.59 percentage points** for few-shot (C3). This observed direction aligns with the hypothesis that synthetic data incurs a larger negative penalty on subjective classification than on objective classification.
- **Contrary Outcome on TREC:** The finding that synthetic data outperformed real training on TREC (+0.0247 to +0.0402 macro-F1) contradicts the naive assumption that synthetic data is universally inferior to human-authored text. Prototypical question framing generated by `qwen3:4b` may align well with standard question-type categories, whereas the 180-row real sample may carry higher sample-specific variance.

#### 2. Few-Shot versus Zero-Shot Synthetic Data (C3 − C2)

- **TREC:** Few-shot synthetic data outperformed zero-shot by **+0.0155 macro-F1** and **+0.0200 accuracy** (+10 test questions correct).
- **Emotion:** Few-shot synthetic data yielded **+0.0037 macro-F1** but **-0.0020 accuracy** (-4 test tweets correct). The two metrics disagree in sign.
- **Demonstration Costs:** Few-shot generation required 59 distinct real pool demonstrations for TREC and 54 for Emotion, whereas zero-shot required 0. In Emotion, this demonstration expenditure produced an ambiguous, negligible shift (+0.0037 F1, -0.0020 accuracy) on a handful of test items.

#### 3. Real + Few-Shot Mix versus Real-Only (C4 − C1)

- **TREC:** The 50/50 mix (90 real + 90 few-shot examples) produced the highest performance in the entire TREC grid: **0.6638 macro-F1** and **0.7100 accuracy**, exceeding real-only C1 by **+0.0629 macro-F1** and **+0.0580 accuracy** (+29 questions correct).
- **Emotion:** The 50/50 mix reached **0.4033 macro-F1** and **0.4425 accuracy**, differing from C1 by **-0.0049 macro-F1** and **+0.0005 accuracy** (+1 tweet correct). The two metrics disagree in sign and sit within single-item sampling noise.

#### 4. AugGPT Paraphrase Augmentation versus Low-Resource Real (C5b − C5a)

- **TREC:** C5b (60 real sources + 360 AugGPT paraphrases = 420 rows) scored **0.6251 macro-F1** and **0.6700 accuracy**, exceeding the low-resource real baseline C5a (60 real sources; **0.5974 macro-F1**, **0.6400 accuracy**) by **+0.0277 macro-F1** and **+0.0300 accuracy** (+15 questions correct).
- **Emotion:** C5b scored **0.3432 macro-F1** and **0.3705 accuracy**, differing from C5a (60 real sources; **0.3381 macro-F1**, **0.3730 accuracy**) by **+0.0051 macro-F1** and **-0.0025 accuracy** (-5 tweets correct).
- **Confounding with Size:** C5b has 420 training rows while C5a has only 60. Because C5b provides a $7\times$ expansion in training rows without requiring additional human labels, any observed difference mixes the effect of AugGPT lexical rephrasing with the effect of raw sample volume.

### Scientific Boundaries and Retained Limitations

Every finding reported above is subject to strict scientific boundaries:

1. **Single-Seed and Single-Run Constraints**:
   All twelve condition results derive from a single seed (`seed 13`). We do not report cross-seed standard deviations, error bars, or statistical confidence intervals, because no estimate of sampling variance exists for a single run. Differences of a few hundredths of macro-F1 or accuracy can stem from sampling noise alone; a different seed could alter the magnitude or sign of any gap. We make **no claims of statistical significance, reproducibility across seeds, or stability**.

2. **Task Confounding (Non-Causal Subjectivity)**:
   TREC and Emotion differ in many characteristics beyond task subjectivity:
   - *Label Semantics*: TREC's six classes represent syntactic question types (abbreviation, entity, description, human, location, numeric), whereas Emotion's six classes represent psychological states (sadness, joy, love, anger, fear, surprise).
   - *Text Length and Style*: TREC contains short search-style questions ($\le 20$ words), whereas Emotion contains informal social media tweets ($\le 30$ words).
   - *Test Set Scale and Balance*: TREC has 500 test questions (with an `abbreviation` class of only 9 items, where a single question shifts macro-F1 by ~0.018), whereas Emotion has 2,000 test tweets (with majority class `joy` accounting for 35% of the data).
   - *Baseline Performance*: Real baseline performance on Emotion is weak (0.4082 macro-F1; 0.4420 accuracy vs a 0.35 majority-class baseline), whereas TREC baseline performance is 0.6009 macro-F1.
   Consequently, **cross-task differences cannot be causally attributed to task subjectivity alone**.

3. **Separate Labeling and Demonstration Budgets**:
   Equal classifier training counts (180 rows in C1–C4) do not represent equal human labeling budgets:
   - C1 uses 180 real labeled rows.
   - C2 uses 0 real labeled rows.
   - C3 uses 180 synthetic rows generated from **59** (TREC) and **54** (Emotion) real labeled demonstrations.
   - C4 uses 90 real rows plus 90 synthetic rows drawing on up to 59/54 real demonstrations.
   - C5b re-uses the 60 real sources from C5a to produce 360 paraphrases without additional labeling.

4. **Model-Assisted Quality Review Limitations**:
   Content audits were conducted using model-assisted screening (Claude Opus 5.5, single pass) and heuristic regex scans. They **must not be described as independent human annotation, multi-annotator consensus, or population accuracy estimates**. Ten-item screening batches serve as a quality gate, not a statistical measurement of dataset error rates.

5. **Disclosed Artifact Quality Flaws (Option A Freeze)**:
   Under lead Option A ([docs/freeze/README.md](../freeze/README.md)), the seed-13 corpora were frozen and accepted with known, disclosed defects:
   - **Emotion AugGPT**: All 12 paraphrases of two long sources (`emotion.train.07790` and `emotion.train.15223`) change the core propositional meaning (reversing the agent from wife to husband; attributing a third-party's strength to the author).
   - **TREC AugGPT (v2)**: Retains ~13 likely problematic rows (corrupted entity names like *"Hot and Wheels"*, label-word leaks like *"Kathie a human"*, one reversed relation, and ~3–4 rows shifting the expected answer type).
   - **TREC Train/Test Duplicate**: Official dataset duplicate `trec.train.05262 = trec.test.00378` (abbreviation) is present in the real training data and retained in C1 and C4 under a lead "pin + disclose" decision.
   - **Cross-Setting Overlap**: 18 identical normalized TREC texts appear in both selected zero-shot and few-shot corpora, reflecting low diversity in question generation.

---

### Conclusion

In this exploratory single-seed comparison (`scope30-v1`), compact local LLM generation (`qwen3:4b`) produced synthetic training data that exhibited starkly contrasting behaviors across task types:
- On the objective TREC question classification task, synthetic data matched or exceeded real-data performance across all conditions (C2, C3, C4, C5b), with a 50/50 real-synthetic mix achieving the highest performance in the grid (+0.0629 macro-F1 over real-only data).
- On the subjective Emotion benchmark, synthetic data consistently underperformed real-data training, with zero-shot and few-shot synthesis incurring negative relative gaps (-6.80% and -5.90% macro-F1), while few-shot demonstrations and AugGPT paraphrasing yielded negligible, ambiguous shifts.

While the observed direction of the synthetic-versus-real gap is consistent with prior findings that LLM synthetic data degrades on subjective domains (Li et al., 2023), our single-seed, two-benchmark design does not allow causal attribution to task subjectivity, nor does it establish statistical stability across random seeds or model architectures. Future work should evaluate multi-seed replications, scale generator capacity, and systematically isolate linguistic and task confounds before drawing general conclusions on the viability of synthetic text classifiers.

## Appendix A. Prompt Templates and Generation Examples

### Part 1: Actual Prompt Templates

The exact prompt generation functions from `ws1_generation/generate.py` are documented below. All prompts enforce valid JSON replies formatted as `{"examples": ["...", "..."]}`.

#### 1. Zero-Shot Prompt Template (`prompt_zero_shot`)

```python
NUDGES = [
    "Pick specific, uncommon subjects.",
    "Mix short and longer examples.",
    "Use a different sentence structure each time.",
    "Avoid repeating the same opening words."
]

def prompt_zero_shot(task, label, n, i):
    t = CFG["tasks"][task]
    if task == "trec":
        boundaries = {
            "abbreviation": "Ask explicitly for an acronym's expansion or a shortened form. "
                            "Do not ask for a general definition or explanation.",
            "entity": "The answer must name a non-human thing, object, product, animal or substance. "
                      "Do not ask for a definition, acronym expansion or chemical symbol.",
            "description": "Ask for an explanation, definition, reason or how something works. "
                           "Do not ask what an acronym stands for.",
            "human": "The answer must name a person, team, group or organisation. "
                     "Mentioning a person does not suffice when the requested answer is their work or creation.",
            "location": "The answer must be a geographic place such as a city, country, mountain or river. "
                        "Do not ask for a person, definition, count, age or acronym expansion.",
            "numeric": "The answer must be a number, count, date, age, duration or measurement. "
                       "Do not ask for a product name or a place name.",
        }[label] + " Ask open questions, not yes/no questions."
    else:
        boundaries = {
            "sadness": "Clearly express feeling sad, lonely, hurt or low in a concrete situation.",
            "joy": "Clearly express happiness, delight, contentment or excitement. "
                   "The dominant feeling should be happiness rather than affection toward someone.",
            "love": "Clearly express affection, tenderness or care toward someone or something. "
                    "A good event or feeling happy alone is not sufficient.",
            "anger": "Clearly express anger, irritation, frustration or annoyance about a situation. "
                     "Do not rely on a situation alone without expressing the writer's reaction.",
            "fear": "Clearly express feeling afraid, anxious, nervous or worried about a threat or uncertainty.",
            "surprise": "Clearly express being amazed, shocked or caught off guard by an unexpected event. "
                        "Mentioning a surprise party alone does not establish the writer's reaction.",
        }[label] + " Make this the clear dominant emotion; use varied situations and natural wording."
    return (f"You are building a dataset of {t['context']}.\n"
            f"Write {n} different examples of {t['style']}.\n"
            f'Every example must have the label "{label}", meaning: {t["labels"][label]}.\n'
            f"Requirements for this label: {boundaries}\n"
            f"At most {t['max_words']} words each. {NUDGES[i % len(NUDGES)]}\n"
            "Check each candidate against the requested label before returning it. "
            "Return only examples that clearly fit; do not include answers or explanations.\n"
            'Reply only with JSON: {"examples": ["...", "..."]}')
```

#### 2. Few-Shot Prompt Template (`prompt_few_shot`)

The few-shot prompt inherits the zero-shot task framing, label definition, and boundaries, inserting three real demonstrations from the isolated demonstration pool split:

```python
def prompt_few_shot(task, label, n, examples, i):
    shown = "\n".join(f"- {json.dumps(e)}" for e in examples)
    return (prompt_zero_shot(task, label, n, i).replace(
        "Reply only", f"Here are real examples with this label:\n{shown}\n"
        "Use these for style, while following the requested label's requirements above.\n"
        "Write NEW examples in the same style. Do not copy or paraphrase these.\nReply only"))
```

#### 3. AugGPT Paraphrasing Templates

The AugGPT pipeline utilizes three prompt variants depending on task and source length:

**Base AugGPT Template (`prompt_auggpt`):**
```python
def prompt_auggpt(text, k):
    return (f"Please rephrase the following sentence {k} different ways, keeping its meaning:\n"
            f"{json.dumps(text)}\n"
            'Reply only with JSON: {"examples": ["...", "..."]}')
```

**Length Guard Variant (`prompt_auggpt_length_guard`):**
Used when source length exceeds `max_words` (30 words in Emotion):
```python
def prompt_auggpt_length_guard(text, k, max_words):
    return prompt_auggpt(text, k).replace(
        "Reply only",
        f"Each rephrasing must be at most {max_words} words, so shorten it: keep the main "
        "meaning and the writer's feeling, and leave out minor details.\nReply only")
```

**Corrected TREC Answer-Type Template (`prompt_auggpt_trec_answer_type`, ADR 0013 v2):**
Replaces the base prompt for all TREC seed-13 AugGPT sources. Calls send `{"think": false, "num_predict": 1024}`:
```python
def prompt_auggpt_trec_answer_type(text, k, label, definition, max_words):
    label_rule = {
        "abbreviation": "If it asks what an abbreviation or acronym stands for, every rephrasing must still "
                        "ask what it stands for (its expansion), not for a definition, meaning or explanation "
                        "of the thing. If it asks for the abbreviation of something, every rephrasing must "
                        "still ask for that abbreviation.",
        "description": "Every rephrasing must remain a question asking for an explanation, definition, "
                       "reason or how something is done, never one whose answer is a name, person, place, "
                       "number or event.",
        "human": "If it asks who a named person, group or organisation is (for example \"Who is X ?\"), "
                 "every rephrasing must still ask who that same person, group or organisation is; it keeps "
                 "the human label, so do not turn it into a request for a definition, explanation or "
                 "biography. Otherwise every rephrasing must still expect a person, group of people, "
                 "organisation or title as its answer.",
    }.get(label, f"Every rephrasing must still expect this kind of answer: {definition}.")
    return (f"Please rephrase the following question {k} different ways:\n"
            f"{json.dumps(text)}\n"
            f'Its answer-type label is "{label}", meaning: {definition}.\n'
            "Every rephrasing must keep the same expected answer type, so it keeps this label. "
            f"{label_rule}\n"
            "Keep the same facts and relationships between them, the same entities and participants "
            "(who did what to whom), any negation, and the same intent.\n"
            "Each rephrasing must be a question or request for information. Do not answer the question, "
            "and do not add facts, names, dates or details that are not in it.\n"
            f"Each rephrasing must be at most {max_words} words; if the question is longer, word it more "
            "concisely without dropping any of its facts.\n"
            'Reply only with JSON: {"examples": ["...", "..."]}')
```
